# Review of the trending-signal brief (2026-09-15)

A second look at `NEXT_SESSION.md` and the movers findings in `README.md`,
done before writing any app code. Everything below was re-measured on the
panel rebuilt today (469,659 card-months after the 09-15 nightly backfilled
full sale histories for the top-60 roster; the earlier findings were measured
on 422,595 rows with those histories capped at 200 sales). Scripts:
`catchup_study.py`, `char_weekly.py`, `data_checks.py`, plus the existing
`backtest.py`. Market facts were checked against press releases and hobby
news (links at the end).

## 1. What the brief got wrong

### 1a. "Character catch-up" is not a tradable signal

The brief's headline (hot-character laggards earn +13.4% / +3.9% excess over
60–90 days vs +8.4% / +0.8% for quiet characters) compares hot laggards with
*all* cards in quiet characters. Quiet-character laggards are the worst group
in every cut, so pooling them into the baseline manufactures the premium. The
honest comparison is against quiet-character **leaders**, with months as the
unit of inference (card-months inside one month all share the same market):

English-language universe (what PokeSniper shows), 60–90 day tradable target,
excess over the universe median, 13% fee for "net":

| group | n | mean excess | median excess | net | doubled | t vs quiet-leader | months > 0 |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| hot laggard, active (sold in last 30d) | 1,530 | +11.2% | +1.6% | +5% | 6.6% | 0.7 | 50% |
| hot laggard, stale (no sale in 30d) | 134 | +15.0% | +4.4% | +9% | 7.5% | 0.9 | 53% |
| hot leader | 2,344 | +9.8% | +1.7% | +5% | 6.3% | −1.2 | 42% |
| quiet laggard | 6,822 | +7.1% | +0.2% | −3% | 4.0% | −1.9 | 37% |
| quiet leader | 3,979 | +9.4% | +2.1% | +1% | 4.9% | — | — |

Nothing here clears t = 2. Across 72 threshold combinations (hot 20–50%,
"up" 10–30%, laggard 0–10%, character size 3–5) the hot-laggard minus
quiet-leader gap ranges from −3 to +2 points. "Positive every year 2022–2026"
rests on 2–20 hot laggards a year before 2024. At 150–180 days the active hot
laggards reach t = 2.1 (+22% vs +16% mean, +10% vs +4% median), but the whole
effect sits in 2025–26, one regime.

The movers study left no script, so its exact numbers cannot be reproduced;
on the rebuilt panel the all-cards hot-laggard mean is +8–14%, not +13.4%.

Two things in that table *are* worth keeping: the stale hot laggards (a card
nobody has traded in a month inside a character that is moving) are the best
cell by median at both horizons, and quiet laggards are reliably the worst.
Both are small.

### 1b. The regime gauge has no lead; its correlation is the 2021→2026 trend

Confirmed: doubling within 60–90 days is common in the 2025–26 boom (6.3% of
tradable card-months since 2025-06; 12–15% in Jan–Mar 2026), and the monthly
doubling rate is persistent (autocorrelation 0.76). Not confirmed: that
market breadth (share of cards up >20% over 30 days) *predicts* it. The raw
correlation of breadth with the next-quarter doubling rate is +0.36 and with
the universe's forward median return +0.50, but the universe median forward
return went −15% (2021), −6%, −4%, +4%, +10%, +18% (2026) while breadth
climbed 9% → 26%. Remove the yearly mean and the correlations turn
**negative** (−0.25 and −0.15); inside 2025–26 alone they are −0.24 and
−0.14. Breadth peaked in May 2026 (33%) exactly as forward returns went
negative. A high-breadth reading is a description of the last month, and at
extremes it has behaved like a top, not a green light.

### 1c. The event attribution is loose, and the dates matter

The brief credits the Jan–Mar 2026 doublers to "the 30th Celebration and Mega
Evolution announcements". Checked against the record:

- Pikachu (49 doublers in Feb 2026): Goldin announced Logan Paul's Illustrator
  auction on 2 Jan, opened it 5 Jan, and sold it for $16.5M on 16 Feb, with
  Pokémon Day (27 Feb) and the 30th anniversary on top. Pikachu breadth
  crossed 30% on 12 Jan, seven days after the auction opened; the median
  Pikachu card bought that week returned +50% over 60–90 days, and +58–62%
  for entries in early/mid February. By mid-April forward returns were
  negative.
- Rayquaza: the Storm Emeralda / "Emerald Surge" leak cycle ran from a June
  2025 trademark through a 29 Sep 2025 leak to the official Mega Rayquaza ex
  reveal on 18 Jun 2026 (JP release 31 Jul, English Delta Reign 6 Nov).
  Rayquaza breadth was 31–43% around the September leak and buyers then lost
  18–23% over 60–90 days; it was 66–72% in Apr–May 2026 and buyers lost 5–25%.
- Mew / Mewtwo / Umbreon / Greninja / Salamence: 30th Celebration was
  announced 27 Feb 2026 and fully revealed 1 Jun 2026. Their breadth was
  50–61% in April; from the reveal date forward returns were −4% to −18%.
- Charizard (Jan 2026): a $550k 1st Edition sale at Heritage in Dec 2025 and
  a $954,800 sale in Feb 2026; Charizard breadth was only 9–11% in Nov–Dec
  2025 while the median card bought then returned +14–48%. Breadth crossed
  30% on 9 Mar 2026, after most of the move.
- Umbreon / Eevee (2025): Prismatic Evolutions was announced 1 Nov 2024 and
  released 17 Jan 2025. Umbreon breadth stayed 11–18% through December while
  the median card bought then returned +27–43%; breadth crossed 30% on 13
  Jan 2025 (73 days after the announcement), by which point the forward
  return had fallen to +13% and then +8%.

So character moves are event-driven, fast, and often front-run by leaks.
Breadth ≥30% fires anywhere from one week to ten weeks after the news, and
breadth ≥50% has marked tops. That is the main reason the catch-up rule
cannot work as stated: by the time 30% of a character is up 20%, the
laggards' remaining upside depends on which stage of the event you are in.

Measured on the panel (English, 60–90d; `catchup_study.py --english --stage`),
by stage of character heat, median excess return and median net return:

| stage at t | laggards: median excess / median net | leaders: median excess / median net | n |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| warming (≥30% now, 20–30% last month) | +6.9% / +4.2% | +4.0% / +3.3% | 363 / 630 |
| newly hot (≥30% now, <20% last month) | +4.7% / −2.4% | +2.7% / +0.5% | 783 / 781 |
| stayed hot (≥30% both months) | +1.9% / −6.8% | −1.4% / −9.4% | 518 / 933 |
| not hot | +0.2% / −9.3% | +2.2% / −5.1% | 6,822 / 3,979 |

The ordering (warming > newly hot > stayed hot) holds for both laggards and
leaders at 60–90 days and matches every event trace above; at 150–180 days
the stages stop separating (medians +8% to +15% for every hot stage), which
is the 2025–26 boom lifting everything. None of the 60–90 day gaps is
statistically strong on its own (n is a few hundred per cell), and only the
"warming" cell is net positive at the median after the 13% fee.

### 1d. The sale-visibility lag is a non-issue

The brief said the lag was unmeasured and that sub-monthly effects should be
distrusted. Measured today (seed scrape of 09-11 vs the full snapshot of
09-12/13, restricted to 5,703 assets far below the 200-sale cap in both
runs): 0–1% of sales dated two or more days before a scrape were missing from
it, all eBay; auction-house sales appear at once. alt.xyz is effectively
real-time for our purposes. (A naive diff of the two runs shows 40k "late"
sales up to 73 days old; that is the 200-sale cap of the laptop run, not a
lag. The 09-12 laptop run was capped despite the README; only the GitHub
runs are uncapped.)

### 1e. Fees: 13% is the sell side only

Buy-It-Now sales are 17% above auction hammer for the same card in the same
month (median over 99k card-months; 25% below $100, 10% at $100–500, 7% at
$500–2k, 4.5% above $2k). "Buy at the first sale after t" assumes you won
that auction at hammer. A realistic round trip for a $100–500 card is closer
to 23% (13.25% eBay final value fee to $7,500 plus the entry premium), which
turns every 60–90 day "net" figure in the brief negative. Auction-house
routes (PWCC/Fanatics vault) have different schedules; the point is the fee
model needs a tier-dependent entry premium, not a flat 13%.

### 1f. Universe and coverage

- 42% of panel rows are Japanese-language cards (12,956 of 45,162 assets).
  PokeSniper's server drops them, so anything meant for the app has to be
  measured on the English universe (`catchup_study.py --english`).
- 598 non-roster assets are still capped at their newest 200 sales (the
  seed run's cap; all top-60 assets were backfilled by the 09-15 nightly).
  The weekly full run fixes it. The early-year panel is also thin because
  alt.xyz's own coverage grew: 97k sales in 2021 vs 792k in 2026 to date.
- The `dbl` column of the earlier regime table divided by all rows, not rows
  with a target; the corrected series is in `data_checks.py`.

## 2. What does hold (refreshed on the rebuilt panel)

| signal | 60–90d IC (t, months >0) | 150–180d IC (t, months >0) | top-decile net 60–90d / 150–180d |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| character spillover, `char_mom30` (continuous) | +0.030 (4.6, 73%) | +0.032 (4.9, 74%) | −6.8% / −2.0% |
| volume surge, `vol_surge` | −0.047 (−7.0, 19%) | −0.070 (−9.1, 13%) | −13.4% / −12.0% |
| price momentum, `mom30` | +0.014 (2.2, 62%) | +0.010 (1.4, 62%) | −7.5% / −3.1% |
| walk-forward GBM (19 features) | +0.048 (3.2, 65%) | +0.102 (5.7, 77%) | −1.7% / +13.4% |
| universe (buy everything) | — | — | −7.4% / +7.5% |

- **Character spillover is real but small**: a continuous rank on "how much
  the character's other cards moved" is right in three months of four, at
  both horizons, and got stronger once the roster's full histories were in.
  It ranks cards; it does not pay for fees on a 2–3 month hold.
- **A volume burst is the most reliable thing in the data, and it is a
  warning**: cards whose last-30-day sale count jumped relative to their
  yearly average underperform in 81–87% of months. This is the app's
  "volume rising before price" thesis with the sign flipped, and it fits how
  supply works (a spike in sales is graded copies arriving, not buyers).
- The 6-month fitted model still leans on card age, character breadth and
  low turnover (vintage premium 2024–26). Its +13% top-decile net is an
  absolute number in a boom; the universe made +7.5% net over the same
  windows.
- PSA 10 population moves fast enough to measure: +0.82% in the four days
  between the 09-11 and 09-15 snapshots (1.2% for 2023+ cards, 0.5% for
  2017–22, 0.18% for WOTC). Pop velocity per card will be usable as a
  feature within about two months, and it is the natural supply-side
  counterpart to the volume warning.

## 3. Research theories to work through before anything ships

Ordered by expected value. Each has a concrete test and a kill criterion.

**T1. Events are the signal; breadth is the echo.** Build the announcement
calendar the brief's task 4 describes, but with *first public information*
dates (leak, trademark, official reveal, release, record sale), not just
release dates, and with featured Pokémon per event. Measure the character
index (`char_weekly.py`, weekly, per character) in the 8 weeks before and 16
after each event, by event type. Questions: how much of the move happens
before the official date (front-running by leaks), how long the drift lasts,
whether reveal/release days are "sell the news" (the June 2026 reveals say
yes). Sources: Bulbapedia set pages (announcement and release dates),
PokeBeach (leak dates), press.pokemon.com, Pokémon Presents dates (27 Feb
every year plus summer). Kill criterion: if post-announcement 60–90 day
excess returns for featured characters are not positive in most events
outside 2025–26, the event pipeline is a news feed, not a signal.

**T2. Record sales as self-contained events.** Our own sales file contains
the headline prints ($550k Charizard Dec 2025, $954,800 Feb 2026, Umbreon
Gold Star $104,920 Apr 2026 then $168,000 Jun 2026, $85,000 EX Deoxys
Rayquaza Aug 2026). Define an event as a sale ≥1.5× the card's own reference
on a card that is one of the character's top-5 by price, and test whether the
character's *other* cards drift afterwards. No external data needed, dated to
the day, and it is exactly the mechanism behind the Pikachu and Charizard
episodes.

**T3. Stage-aware spillover.** Keep the continuous `char_mom30` rank but gate
it on stage: breadth rising through 20–45% counts, breadth ≥50% or a second
consecutive hot month does not. Test whether the gate lifts the IC and, more
importantly, the median net return of the top decile at 150–180 days. The
hypothesis is a hump: early heat is followed by drift, late heat by
reversal. Kill criterion: gated IC not above ungated IC with t ≥ 2.5.

**T4. Volume surge and pop velocity as an avoid list.** The negative
volume-surge result is strong enough to ship as a warning today. Extend it
with pop velocity once 8–12 weeks of daily snapshots exist: (PSA 10 pop
growth over 30 days) ÷ (sales over 30 days) as a supply-pressure ratio.
Prediction: cards whose pop grows faster than their sales absorb it
underperform their character. This is the only signal on the list whose
mechanism does not depend on the 2025–26 regime.

**T5. Modern-to-vintage transmission.** When a character's modern chase
cards rise (new set pull hype), do its vintage cards follow? The cross-era
cut today gives t = 3.2 for vintage laggards under modern heat at 150–180
days, but it is 2025 (the vintage premium year) doing the work. Re-test
inside T1 with event dates, so "a new set featured this Pokémon" is separated
from "vintage went up in 2025".

**T6. An execution model instead of a flat fee.** Tier-dependent entry
premium (from the BIN/auction spread above), eBay's 13.25% to $7,500 then
2.35%, and a holding-period floor. Re-run every table net of that. If a
signal's median net return at 150–180 days is not positive under this model
for the $100–2k tiers (where most roster cards trade), it does not go near
money.

**T7. Market temperature, described not predicted.** Publish breadth,
doubling rate and median 30-day move as a gauge with the plain statement
that it has no measured lead. Test the contrarian version (breadth ≥30% for
two months → next-quarter universe return) once there are more than 63
months to work with; today it is suggestive and nothing more.

## 4. What this means for what ships first

- Do not ship "Hot / Rising / Flat / Cooling" as a buy signal built from
  breadth thresholds. If Sid wants a character-level label now, define it by
  stage (warming / newly hot / stayed hot / cooling) and print the measured
  base rates from the stage table above next to it, including the negative
  ones.
- The volume-surge warning ("sales jumped, prices usually follow down") is
  the one thing with the statistics to back a per-card flag today.
- Card-level "trend score" = the continuous character-spillover rank, shown
  as relative strength with the fee caveat, not as an expected return.
- The event pipeline (T1/T2) is the research priority, because every large
  move in the last two years traces to a dated piece of news, and the only
  signal that gave lead time on those moves was the news itself.
